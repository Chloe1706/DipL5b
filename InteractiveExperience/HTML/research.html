Research Practical
Technical Research

Over the Easter break; following the Visual Programming project, I was eager to experiment more with Touch Designer. In particular, I wanted to try movement/hand tracking using the Media Pipe plugin as I did not get the opportunity to try this during the project. I followed some tutorials by Torin Blankensmith, half of the duo who created and maintain the plugin.

Given that the brief is to create an interactive experience, I thought that using hand tracking data to influence visuals would be a good approach.

GitHub. (2024). google-ai-edge/mediapipe. [online] Available at: https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe.

When discussing latency issues in my Visual Programming project, I mentioned how I was keen to test Ableton Live as this, I believe, is a more industry standard approach to incorporate live audio data with Touch Designer. This led me onto the idea of combining this with Media Pipe and effectively using the hand tracking data as a midi input to drive audio in Ableton Live. I’ve seen some examples of this online however it was difficult to find a good tutorial. Fortunately, I had the opportunity to ask Jojo during a workshop and he demonstrated to me how to do this.

Research Case Study
Silencing the Virus — Interactive exhibition, Lily Hunter Green
Case Study Research

Once I had decided to create an interactive experience using the methods outlined above, I began to consider how the project could be presented.

From an early stage, I wanted to avoid making the work purely screen-based. Instead, I aimed to integrate my background in fine art; particularly my interest in sculpture and physical media, into the project. I also anticipated that many other students on the Creative Computing diploma would produce screen-based work, so I wanted to differentiate my project and contribute to a greater variety of outcomes within the Creative Computing exhibition at Gradfest.

I thought of Lily Hunter Green, an Interdisciplinary artist and guest lecturer on the 19th of March. I really liked how her work has a larger sense of presence in-part due to the use of lighting and physical structures in combination with digital media. I also thought of the piece ‘The Day We Left Field’ by Tundra which I wrote about in the Visual Programming project. These works exemplify how it is possible to move beyond screen-based digital art by embedding interactive technology within physical forms, emphasising spatial engagement and the artworks physical presence.

I decided that I wanted to do something similar so began to consider how I could incorporate something physical into a work which utilised Touch Designer, hand tracking and interactive visuals and sound. This led me to consider projection mapping using Mad Mapper and creating something which the projections could be aligned to.

Research Contextual/Ideation
Tulip Fields - Lisse, Netherlands

Interactive Experience

Mindmap

I was initially drawn to creating a more organic, physical form to combine with TouchDesigner visuals through projection mapping. However, I was conscious of avoiding something that too closely referenced The Day We Left Field by TUNDRA.

During a conversation with someone, the idea of using flowers was suggested. This reminded me of a fairly recent trip to the Netherlands where I visited and documented the tulip arrangements at Keukenhof, Lisse. This became the starting point for my contextual research behind this project.

Hybridisation

Selective breeding of tulips

Keukenhof is one of the world's largest flower gardens, open for only eight weeks out of the year and is best known for its diverse spectrum of Tulip bulb varieties, a national symbol of Dutch Heritage.

One of the attractions that I was most drawn to were the historical gardens. These drew attention to the act of cross-pollination and hybridisation by human intervention, which has been evidenced throughout history.

Tulipa displays - Keukenhof, 2025
Contextual Research

In the 16th and 17th Century, tulip bulbs were transported to Western Europe. This sparked the "Tulipomania" in the Netherlands which prompted botanists to dedicate their efforts to breeding and spreading variegation; which is the appearance of different colours in a streaking effect caused by mutation.

There is something about the human effort to manufacture results in nature that I find interesting.

In my research for the Visual Programming project, I made various mentions of how works which utilise natural imagery and technology create a sense of disparity and this can succinctly convey messages that speak to environmental uncertainty and the human impact on the natural world.

I find the approach and consistency with which humans have been hybridising tulips throughout history fascinating and ultimately, this premise has formed the underlying contextual basis for my Interactive Experience project.

Sketching and Ideation

Combining this contextual research with the concepts from earlier, I arrived at a final idea for my Interactive Experience project.

Ideation

Using hand tracking I planned to create an interactive artwork where the user could wave their hand across a bed of white tulips and cause them to light up with different colours. I knew that when it came to show this work at Gradfest there would be a lot of interactive work. With this in mind, I had a few key goals with this project:

Casual Engagement

I wanted the work to be uncomplicated and approachable to curate a more casual style of engagement. I did not want screens, menus, controllers, headsets or just about any other gear that could make the work feel less casual.

To me, it was important that people could walk up to it and get it straight away.

Accessible

- I wanted the work to be suitable for a wide range of people, including children, elderly, those who speak different languages and those with neurodevelopmental conditions and SPLDs.

Simplicity

- This mostly facilitates the first two but also; and I often forget this myself, there is an elegance to things which are conceptually simple. As I have worked on various projects during the diploma up to this point, there has been the temptation to try more complicated ideas. For this project I wanted to keep it technically more simple so that I could ensure I’d have enough time to execute it to a high standard.
